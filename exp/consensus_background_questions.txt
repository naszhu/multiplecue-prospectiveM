Consensus Briefing: MCP + PM Experiment Background and Questions

Purpose of the current experiment
- The project tests how prospective memory (PM) intentions compete with ongoing value-based intention selection in a multiple-cue paradigm (MCP/CCRP).
- The central theoretical goal is to evaluate whether PM costs are best explained by:
  1) shared-capacity competition inside MIS,
  2) threshold/control adjustments outside MIS, or
  3) a hybrid account.
- Key comparison: baseline vs focal PM vs nonfocal PM, with both choice and RT outcomes.

Core design summary (from methods draft)
- Ongoing task: on each trial, participants see 3 colored circles arranged in a triangle and choose by color key (N/J/K mapping).
- Reward structure: single-cue trials (one rewarded option) and dual-cue trials (two rewarded options with different values).
- PM manipulation:
  - Focal PM cue: one circle turns orange.
  - Nonfocal PM cue: one circle gets a dashed outline.
  - PM response is spacebar (instead of ongoing color response) when PM cue appears.
- Within-subject blocked design:
  - Baseline block(s): no PM task.
  - Focal PM block(s).
  - Nonfocal PM block(s).
  - PM cue frequency in PM blocks is 15%.
- Main dependent measures:
  - Ongoing-task accuracy and RT,
  - PM hit rate and false alarms,
  - PM monitoring cost (non-PM trial RT in PM blocks minus matched baseline RT).

Training/time context for interpreting keyboard-mapping effects
- Methods draft states a long protocol (~8-9 total hours) across multiple sessions and extensive training before main PM testing.
- User’s current protocol concern also emphasizes very long training exposure (up to many sessions; user notes 17 sessions in current planning), which may reduce reliance on visible key aids over time.

Why this matters for my next search
- I need evidence-based guidance on whether physically adding color-key mapping to keyboard keys changes behavior (beneficial scaffolding vs attentional distraction/confound), especially in long multi-session protocols with substantial training.
